Consultation for paralysis, repeated or sudden unconsciousness, epilepsy, facial weakness, or frothing from the mouth.

Your visit

What happens, step by step

A first visit usually takes about 20 to 30 minutes. Knowing what to expect makes the day easier, especially if you are bringing an elderly parent or a child.

Send your details

Message the clinic on WhatsApp with the patient's name, age, and a short note about the concern. You will get a confirmed slot for the coming Wednesday.

Bring past records

Carry any earlier prescriptions, MRI or CT films, EEG reports, and the current medicine strips or a photo of them. Old records save time and repeat tests.

Consultation and plan

Dr. Ahmed examines the patient, explains what is likely happening, and writes a plan — medicines, any tests needed, and when to come back.

Common questions

Before you come in

Do I need an appointment, or can I walk in?

Walk-ins are seen when there is room, but Wednesday slots fill up. Sending a WhatsApp message in advance means a shorter wait and a confirmed time.

What should I bring to the visit?

Earlier prescriptions, any MRI, CT or EEG reports and films, a list or photo of current medicines, and the patient's ID if available.

Can I message on behalf of a family member?

Yes. Most messages come from family members. Please include the patient's name and age, and say how you are related to them.

Which languages can I use?

Hindi or English, on WhatsApp and in the consultation room. Write whichever is easier for you.

Is the clinic accessible for elderly patients?

Yes. The clinic is on Khagaul Road in Phulwari Sharif with vehicle access close to the entrance. Message us beforehand if the patient uses a wheelchair so we can keep a suitable slot.

Do you give advice over WhatsApp?

WhatsApp is used for appointments and simple queries such as timings and directions. Diagnosis and medicines need an in-person consultation.
